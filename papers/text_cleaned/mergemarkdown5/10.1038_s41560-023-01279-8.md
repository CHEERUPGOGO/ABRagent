# Materials preparation  $\mathsf { } _ { 6 } \mathsf { } _ { 5 } \mathsf { }$ powder was obtained by mechanically ball milling a stoichiometric mixture of Li2S (Alfa, $9 9 . 9 \%$ ), ${ \bf P } _ { 2 } \mathsf { S } _ { 5 }$ (Macklin, $9 9 \%$ ), and LiCl $( 9 9 . 9 \%$ , Aladdin) for 20 h at 600 r.p.m. under an argon atmosphere, followed by sintering at $5 5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for 5 h in a sealed glass ampul. The silicon particles were obtained from Tianmu Energy Anode Materials Co. and used after ball milling. The hard carbon (HC) was obtained from HiNa Battery Technology Co., used as received for characterization and after ball milling for fabrication of the LiHC anode. Soft carbon (SC) was obtained from HiNa Battery Technology Co. and used as received. The graphite (Gr) was obtained from Tianmu Lake Institute of Advanced Energy Storage Technologies and used as received. The SH powders (SH82, SH64, SH46, SH28) were prepared by mechanically ball milling of Si and HC with weight ratio of 8:2, 6:4, 4:6, 2:8, respectively. The SS46 and SG 46 were prepared by ball milling Si with SC and Gr in a weight ratio of 4:6, respectively. The polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) binder used for the cathode and anode was obtained from Daikin Industries and used as received. Vapour grow carbon fibre (VGCF) used for the cathode composite was obtained from Showa Denko K.K. and used as received. $\operatorname { } _ { 1 . 5 } \mathsf { } _ { 0 . 5 } \mathsf { } _ { 1 . 5 } ( \mathsf { } _ { 4 } ) _ { 3 } ( \mathsf { L } i$ ATP)-coated Lithium nickel cobalt manganese oxide (NMC811) was obtained from Beijing Welion New Energy Technology Co. via fluid-bed technology. $\mathsf { } _ { 2 } \mathsf { } _ { 3 } .$ -protected $\mathsf { } _ { 2 }$ was prepared by sol-gel method based on ${ \bf { } } _ { 2 }$ coating, detailed in previous works34.

The Si anode was prepared by repeated rolling of the mixture of Si and PTFE with a weight ratio of 95:5 to a uniform film with loading of $- 2 . 1 \mathsf { } ^ { - 2 }$ ; then the film was punched to discs with a diameter of $1 0 \mathsf { }$ . To develop the Li–Si alloy-based anode and LiHC anode, Si-contained films and HC film were obtained first by repeated rolling the mixture of ball-milled powders and PTFE with a weight ratio of 95:5. The films were punched to discs with diameters of $1 0 \mathrm { }$ for cell assembly. The films used against the NMC811 cathode controlled the weight in a constant capacity 6.5 mAh cm−2. The weight and capacity are shown in Supplementary Table 1. When using the LCO cathode, the loading of SH46 was $- 2 . 2 \mathsf { } ^ { - 2 }$ . Li foil was pouched to discs with diameters of $8 \mathsf { }$ , and the weight of Li foil was dependent on the Li/ Si and Li/SH capacity ratio; in a typical cell, the Li/Si or Li/SH capacity ratio is above 0.8. The thickness of the Li foil is around $3 5 \mu \mathrm { m }$ in ASSBs with NMC811. The Li-contained anodes were in situ formed by short contact between Si-contained films and Li foils during cell assembly. During cycles, the reduction of PTFE may lead to anode structure degradation and Li loss due to PTFE decomposition. However, LiSH anodes were used in ASSBs where sufficient stress and sintering of Si can maintain the structure of the anodes. Additionally, pre-doping lithium can supplement Li loss. Moreover, PTFE is a commonly used binder with low cost in dry electrode preparation, which is easier to control the thickness of Si-contained films.

# Phase field simulation  A phase field with a nucleation model is introduced to simulate the transition of Li+ /Li. In the charging process, Li ion is either embedded in the electrode or deposited as dendrites into the electrolyte, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 16. The three main pathways are considered in the model to describe the relative reactions: $$ 1 5 \mathrm {L i} ^ {+} + 4 \mathrm {S i} + 1 5 \mathrm {e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm {L i} _ {1 5} \mathrm {S i} _ {4} E _ {1} ^ {\ominus} = 0. 0 5 \mathrm {V} \tag {1} $$ $$ \mathrm {L i} ^ {+} + 6 \mathrm {C} + 6 \mathrm {e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm {L i C} _ {6} E _ {1} ^ {\ominus} = 0. 0 0 1 \mathrm {V} \tag {2} $$ $$ \mathrm {L i} ^ {+} + \mathrm {e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm {L i} E _ {2} ^ {\ominus} = 0. 0 0 \mathrm {V} \tag {3} $$ The second reaction (equation (2)) is the diffusion-driven reaction where the Li atom inserts into the anode. The lithium dendrite presents in the third transition path. Three independent non-conserved order parameters $\scriptstyle { \vec { \xi } } = ( \xi _ { 0 } , \xi _ { 1 } , \xi _ { 2 } )$ denote the normalized concentration of species considered in the model. The value of ⃗ξ (0, 0, 0), (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0) and (0, 0, 1) represents the states of electrolyte, lithium metal, $\mathrm { } _ { 6 }$ and Si electrode, respectively. In the model, the Li will transport from the near interface region towards the deeper electrode region with a diffusion-like pattern. The Li atom is in the form of LiC6 and ${ \mathsf { } } _ { 1 5 } { \mathsf { } } _ { 4 }$ inside the electrode. On the contrary, the Li atom at the surface of the anode tends to form Li metal dendrite, damaging ASSBs’ health and leading to short circuiting. Assuming that the first reaction (equation (1)) occurs only at the interface between the electrode and the electrolyte, the lithium atoms are transported inside the carbon electrode by coupling and decoupling with the hard carbon. In the initial state, there are no lithium dendrites, while nucleation leads to the formation of small dendrites, and the dendrites grow during the charging process. The evolution of the process obeys two key equations derived from the nonlinear phase field model: $$ \frac {\partial \xi_ {1}}{\partial t} = - L _ {\sigma 1} \left(\frac {\partial f _ {0}}{\partial \xi_ {1}} - \kappa_ {1} \nabla^ {2} \xi_ {1}\right) + L _ {\eta 1} h ^ {\prime \left(\xi_ {1}\right)} \tag {4} $$ $$ h \left(\xi_ {2}\right) \left[ \exp \frac {\left(1 - \alpha_ {1}\right) F \left(\eta - E _ {1} ^ {\ominus}\right)}{R T} - \frac {c _ {\mathrm {L i}} ^ {+}}{c _ {0}} \exp \frac {- \alpha_ {1} F \left(\eta - E _ {1} ^ {\ominus}\right)}{R T} \right] $$ $$ \frac {\partial \xi_ {0}}{\partial t} = - L _ {\sigma 0} \left(\frac {\partial f _ {0}}{\partial \xi_ {0}} - \kappa_ {0} \nabla^ {2} \xi_ {0}\right) - L _ {\eta 0} h ^ {\prime} (\xi_ {0}) \left[ \exp \frac {(1 - \alpha_ {0}) F \eta}{R T} - \frac {c _ {\mathrm {L i} ^ {+}}}{c _ {0}} \exp \frac {- \alpha_ {0} F \eta}{R T} \right] \tag {5} $$ where $\scriptstyle L _ { \sigma 0 }$ and $L _ { \sigma 1 }$ are the interfacial mobility for $\xi _ { 0 }$ and $\xi _ { 1 } ,$ , respectively; $f _ { 0 }$ s the loctions free energy density, cross terms given by: $\begin{array} { r } { f _ { 0 } = \sum _ { \underline { { i } } } A \breve { \xi _ { i } } ^ { 2 } ( 1 - \xi _ { i } ) ^ { 2 } + \sum _ { i } \sum _ { j \neq i } \frac { B } { 2 } \xi _ { i } ^ { 2 } \xi _ { j } ^ { 2 } } \end{array}$ $L _ { \eta 0 }$ $L _ { \eta 1 }$ $h \left( \xi \right) = \xi ^ { 3 } \left( 6 \xi ^ { 2 } - 1 5 \xi + 1 0 \right)$ is the interpolation function; $\alpha _ { i }$ is the cathodic charge-transfer coefficients for reaction i; $\eta$ is the activation overpotential; $E _ { 1 } ^ { \Theta }$ is the standard half-cell potential vs Li+ /Li; $c _ { \mathrm { } } ^ { + }$ is the concentration of Li+ ; $c _ { 0 }$ is the site density of the electrolyte; and R, T and $F$ are the gas constant, the temperature and the Faraday constant, respectively. The nucleation process is restricted to occurring only near the interface. The nucleation process is described by a series of equations: $$ \delta_ {\mathrm {N}} = \left\{ \begin{array}{l} 1, P _ {\mathrm {N}} = 1 - \exp (- J ^ {*} \Delta t) > \text {r m} [ 0, 1 ] \\ 1, \text {o e} \end{array} \right. \tag {6} $$ $$ J ^ {*} = J _ {0} ^ {*} \exp \left(- \frac {\sigma_ {\text {i o}} \Delta G ^ {*}}{R T}\right) \tag {7} $$ $$ \Delta G ^ {*} = \frac {1 6 \pi \gamma^ {3} V _ {m} ^ {2}}{3 \Delta \mu^ {2}} \tag {8} $$ $$ \Delta \tilde {\mu} = - \ln \frac {\tilde {c} _ {L i +} \tilde {c} _ {e}}{a _ {\mathrm {L i}}} - F (E ^ {\Theta} - \Delta \varphi) \tag {9} $$ where $P _ { \mathrm { { N } } }$ is a Poisson distribution whose expected value is the Li nucleation rate J * . The value of J* is determined by an Arrhenius equation. $J ^ { * } 0$ and $\sigma _ { \mathrm { } }$ are two constant factors, and $\Delta G ^ { * }$ is the activation energy. γ, $\Delta \mu$ and $V _ { m }$ are the chemical potential difference, interfacial, energy and molar volume, respectively. Where $a _ { \mathrm { { } } }$ is the activity of Li metal atom, $E ^ { \Theta }$ is the standard half-cell potential, and Δφ is the interfacial potential difference between the electrode and the electrolyte. For more details, see ref. 35. All the equations are solved by a parallel computational software based on AMRex. The width of the domain is fixed at $1 0 0 \mu \mathrm { m }$ and the length of the domain is computed according to the experimental results by the equation $C = \rho _ { \mathrm { c } } c _ { \mathrm { c } } w _ { \mathrm { i } } + ( 1 - \rho _ { \mathrm { c } } ) c _ { \mathrm { } } w _ { \dot { \nu } }$ where C is a constant to represent the capacity, $c _ { \mathrm { c } }$ and $c _ { \mathrm { } }$ are the capacity of HC and Si, respectively, $\rho _ { \mathrm { c } }$ is the ratio of HC in the electrode, and $w _ { \mathrm { i } }$ is the width of the electrode, which ensures that the total capacity remains constant in all cases. For simplifying the co-effect of HC and Si, the capacity of the battery is the weighted sum of the two components. Six cases with the weight ratios of Si/HC at 0:1, 2:8, 4:6, 6:4, 8:2 and 1:0, denoting LiHC, LiSH28, LiSH46, LiSH64, LiSH82 and LiSi anodes, respectively, are studied in the simulation.
